# LicenceFlow — two-minute hackathon film

This is the recording script for the temporary automatic Raahi build. The Raahi passage is one continuous TTS clip designed to accompany the approximately 45-second automatic tour.

## Full script

**You — to camera**

I wanted to get my Learner’s Licence. The whole process was online, which sounded brilliant: apply from home, pay, take the test.

I watched tutorials, filled every field carefully, and paid. Then the test never began. One required window refused to close, the security software kept blocking me, and the fee was effectively gone. Worse, a technical failure somehow felt like my failure.

That stayed with me. So when I found this hackathon, I knew what to build.

This is LicenceFlow. Its principle is simple: technical failure should never become citizen failure. And it is designed around every small moment where people lose confidence. So let me show you—

**Raahi — from laptop**

Actually… let me.

**You**

Okay, Raahi. Go ahead.

**Turn the laptop towards camera and start the automatic tour. Begin the continuous Raahi clip at the same moment.**

**Raahi — continuous TTS clip**

Hi, I’m Raahi. You should never need a YouTube tutorial to understand a public service. LicenceFlow uses plain English or Hindi, and every screen explains what is happening, why it matters, and what to do next.

The form stays one continuous journey, with recognisable vehicle choices and progress that survives refreshes. Before a rupee is paid, we check the camera, microphone, connection, lighting, and test environment—then rehearse the question interaction.

Payment is never a black box. Its exact state and safe next action stay visible, and we check an earlier attempt before asking you to pay again.

Every confirmed test answer becomes a checkpoint. Even when technology fails, your application, payment, and answers don’t. You resume at the exact next question. Camera signals provide context, never an automatic accusation.

Finally, the result tells you what happened, what it means, and what to do next. LicenceFlow is understandable before action, recoverable after failure, and trustworthy throughout.

**Cut back to you**

And this is only the beginning. I’m building a modern road-safety learning experience and, next, a natural-language guide for anyone who gets stuck.

Because digitising a government form is not enough. A truly digital public service must be understandable, recoverable, and trustworthy.

That’s LicenceFlow.

## Automatic-tour visual beats

- **0–7 seconds — Discover:** homepage, service cards, state choice, Learner’s Licence entry.
- **7–16 seconds — Apply:** all seven form sections, fictional documents, submit.
- **16–23 seconds — Verify:** pre-payment device check and practice question.
- **23–31 seconds — Pay:** fee visibility, simulation consent, gateway, confirmation.
- **31–36 seconds — Learn and test:** tutorial, test instructions, focused question screen.
- **36–42 seconds — Recover:** answer checkpoint, prepared network interruption, safe pause, exact resume.
- **42–49 seconds — Explain:** passing result, recovered status, answer review, reset control.

## TTS direction

Use a warm Indian voice with quiet confidence—not a cartoon voice and not a government announcement. Aim for roughly **165–175 words per minute**. Keep the first sentence lightly playful, the payment section reassuring, and place short emotional emphasis on:

- “Before a rupee is paid”
- “Payment is never a black box”
- “Even when technology fails”
- “never an automatic accusation”
- “understandable… recoverable… trustworthy”

Do not generate the bracketed directions or headings. Generate only the Raahi dialogue as one continuous clip.
